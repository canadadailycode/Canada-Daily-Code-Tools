Hire-Ready Checklist
Affordability & Runway
No. 01

Is this a documented need, or a feeling?

The task is written down. You have a clear, specific description of exactly what this person will do — not a vague sense of "help."
You've done it yourself for at least a month. You've personally run this exact process repeatedly and know how long it takes and what it involves.
It's recurring, not a one-off. This is ongoing, repeatable work — not a single project that will end.
You can estimate the hours it takes weekly. You have a real number, not a guess like "a lot."
The decision isn't driven by this week's overwhelm. You'd still make this hire if you'd had a calm, easy week instead of a stressful one.
You've checked affordability, not just urgency. You know roughly what this hire will cost monthly and have checked it against the calculator in the next tab.
No. 02

Can the business actually carry this cost?

Mandatory employer costs on top of gross salary in Canada typically include CPP employer contributions (matching the employee's ~5.95% up to the yearly maximum), EI employer contributions (1.4× the employee rate), and vacation pay (minimum 4% in most provinces). As a starting rule of thumb, budgeting an extra 20% above gross salary for these mandatory costs is a reasonable estimate — but exact CPP/EI rates, provincial vacation minimums, and WSIB/workers' comp premiums vary and change yearly, so confirm the real number with your accountant or payroll provider before committing.
